## Scope and assessment pattern

This profile is specifically **English Language & Literature (Code 184)**, not English Communicative (101). CBSE's 2026–27 Class X sample-paper listing provides separate entries for both courses. CBSE's April 2026 notification states that Class X students continue the language course taken in Class IX; students who took Communicative in Class IX may continue with it.

The 3-hour written examination is 80 marks, in 11 numbered questions:

| Section | Questions | Marks |
|---|---:|---:|
| A · Reading Skills | Q1–Q2 | 20 |
| B · Grammar and Creative Writing Skills | Q3–Q5 | 20 |
| C · Literature Textbook | Q6–Q11 | 40 |
| **Total** | **11** | **80** |

| Slot | Structure | Marks |
|---|---|---:|
| Q1 | Unseen discursive passage (400–450 words), 8 sub-questions | 10 |
| Q2 | Unseen case-based factual passage (200–250 words) with visual/statistical input, 9 sub-questions | 10 |
| Q3 | 12 grammar tasks; attempt any 10 (1 mark each) | 10 |
| Q4 | Formal letter; one of two options, 100–120 words | 5 |
| Q5 | Analytical paragraph from a map/chart/graph/cues; one of two, 100–120 words | 5 |
| Q6 | First Flight prose/drama extract; one of two extracts | 5 |
| Q7 | First Flight poetry extract; one of two extracts | 5 |
| Q8 | First Flight short answers; attempt any 4 of 5, 40–50 words each | 12 |
| Q9 | Footprints Without Feet short answers; attempt any 2 of 3, 40–50 words each | 6 |
| Q10 | First Flight long answer; one of two, 100–120 words | 6 |
| Q11 | Footprints Without Feet long answer; one of two, 100–120 words | 6 |

The mark distribution is 3 × 10 marks, 4 × 5 marks, 1 × 12 marks and 3 × 6 marks.

## Question-bank design rules

- Q1 and Q2 must use newly written, genuinely unseen passages. Q2 must include a readable table, chart, graph or other data visual; text-only pseudo-data does not qualify.
- Q3 must contain 12 independent, syllabus-tagged tasks covering determiners, tenses, modals, subject–verb concord and reported speech (statements, questions, commands and requests). Any ten must be selectable in the online exam.
- Q4 and Q5 require two valid alternatives, model responses and analytic rubrics.
- Q6 and Q7 require two alternative extracts, each with its own four-part question set and marking key.
- Q8 and Q9 require selectable sub-question groups (4 of 5 and 2 of 3 respectively), with each sub-question independently identified for repeat protection.
- Q10 and Q11 require two alternatives with model responses and mark-by-mark rubrics.
- Every question and every nested item needs a stable content ID. The five-paper protection window must include nested items, not just the parent numbered question.
- Model answers are guidance, not scripts. Rubrics must accept equivalent accurate student expression and must not reward memorised, irrelevant plot summaries.

### Q10 and Q11 content design

- **Q10 — First Flight:** 20 main candidates split evenly between prose/drama (10) and poetry (10). The pool addresses character, theme, authorial choices, situation-based interpretation and textual evidence. Each candidate has a distinct alternative prompt and its own model response and marking scheme.
- **Q11 — Footprints Without Feet:** 20 primary candidates cover A Triumph of Surgery, The Thief's Story, The Midnight Visitor, A Question of Trust, Footprints Without Feet, The Making of a Scientist, The Necklace, Bholi and The Book That Saved the Earth. Each has an internal-choice alternative, model response and marking scheme.
- Every Q10/Q11 model answer (including alternatives) is within 100–120 words; the measured range is 100–115 words.
- Each Q10/Q11 rubric allocates 2 marks for text-specific understanding, 2 for relevant evidence and explanation, 1 for organisation and 1 for accuracy/expression.
- These are internally curated draft-bank candidates. Independent English educator approval and reconciliation with the exact 2026–27 prescribed-text list remain outstanding.

### Q6/Q7 content strategy decision — 5 November 2026

**Decision: retain the current adapted Q6/Q7 passages for the present practice-bank milestone. Park the private/licensed verbatim-extract work as a future enhancement.**

- The repository is public and the GitHub Pages site is publicly accessible. Making the source repository private would not make assets delivered by the published student website private.
- NCERT's online textbook terms state that republication, redistribution in digital content packages/software, and adaptation/summary/derivation are restricted without written permission. See the [NCERT textbook copyright notice](https://epathshala.nic.in/wp-content/doc/book/btextbook/textbook.htm) and [NCERT e-content licence](https://epathshala.nic.in/wp-content/doc/book/gtextbook/textbook.htm).
- Deferred follow-up: if verbatim textbook extracts are required later, obtain written permission from NCERT and define the exact textbooks, chapters/poems, extract lengths, question-bank format, student audience, delivery model and permitted adaptations before implementation.
- If permission is granted, keep licensed source files outside the public GitHub repository and deliver them only through an authenticated content service with access controls. Do not expose signed credentials or private source files in client-side code.
- If permission is declined or unavailable in that future phase, retain original unseen prose and poetry passages for reading/poetic analysis. Label these accurately; do not call them textbook extracts.
- Private GitHub hosting is not itself a copyright licence and is not a substitute for access control on content served to browsers.
